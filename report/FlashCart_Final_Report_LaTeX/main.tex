\chapter{Introduction}

\section{Background and Context}
A modern Indian mall houses dozens of independent showrooms (apparel, electronics, food,
jewellery, beauty), and each runs its own billing counter. A shopper who buys a shirt,
earbuds and a coffee queues three times, pays three times and leaves with three bills, each
with its own GST calculation. \textbf{FlashCart} replaces these counters with one digital
checkout. The shopper signs in with a mobile number and scans the barcode on any product tag
with the phone camera. Every item from every showroom lands in one \emph{unified bag}, which
is paid once (UPI or card through Razorpay, or cash at a central desk) for a single GST
invoice. The final increment closes the loop after the sale: every bill earns
\emph{FlashCart points} that can be spent on the next one, any paid bill can be returned at
the cash desk against a GST credit note, receipts can be shared as a link or on WhatsApp,
and the app installs on the phone with a dark mode.

\subsection{Problem Statement}
Multi-showroom retail suffers from repeated queues and payments per store, fragmented
receipts that are hard to audit for GST, manual cash handling with change errors and no
reliable end-of-day drawer total, and carts that are lost when a device changes. Returns are
handled by hand (no credit note, stock not restored), loyalty schemes are per store, and a
customer has no view of what they spend. The project builds a web-based unified checkout that
removes these problems without new hardware in the showrooms, using the EAN-13 barcodes already
printed on retail tags.

\section{Project Objectives}
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
  \item \textbf{Objective 1:} Phone-number sign-in and camera scanning of EAN-13 / UPC /
        Code 128 barcodes, adding an item to the bag in under one second.
  \item \textbf{Objective 2:} An exact multi-slab GST bill (5\%, 12\%, 18\%) across all
        showrooms, including tags whose MRP already includes GST.
  \item \textbf{Objective 3:} One payment: Razorpay (UPI / card) with server-side HMAC-SHA256
        verification, or cash at a desk.
  \item \textbf{Objective 4:} An email-and-password cash desk that shows the change to return
        and the cash left in the drawer at day end.
  \item \textbf{Objective 5:} Persist all data in a database and cover the business rules
        with automated tests, delivered in four iterations (Section~\ref{sec:sdlc}).
  \item \textbf{Objective 6:} A loyalty scheme (\emph{FlashCart points}) earned on every paid
        bill and redeemable at checkout, and returns at the cash desk with a GST credit note,
        restocking, a cash or card/UPI refund and the points unwound correctly.
  \item \textbf{Objective 7:} A better everyday experience: shareable receipts, a spend summary
        with one-tap re-ordering, several cashiers with their own drawers and shift handover,
        and an installable app with dark mode.
\end{enumerate}

\section{System Architecture}

\subsection{High-Level Design}
FlashCart follows a three-tier client--server architecture
(Figure~\ref{fig:arch}). Two single-page applications run in the browser: the
\emph{customer app} on the shopper's phone, and the \emph{cash desk} on a counter tablet or
laptop. Both talk JSON over HTTPS to one Express REST API, and every protected call carries a
signed bearer token. The API owns all business rules: pricing, stock, payment
verification, cash accounting, points and refunds. Clients never send amounts, they only send
intents. Durable data lives in an SQLite database (now including credit notes and a points
ledger), while short-lived carts and gateway orders are kept in memory. Online payments go
through Razorpay~\cite{razorpay}. The customer app is a progressive web app~\cite{pwa}: a
service worker caches the application shell so it installs on the phone and opens offline,
and receipt share links are signed with HMAC so they open read-only without a sign-in.

  \includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{architecture}

\end{figure}

\subsection{Technical Stack}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
  \item \textbf{Framework/Language:} JavaScript (ES2022). React~19 with Vite~8 for the
        two web apps~\cite{react,vite}; Node.js~22+ with Express~5 for the REST
        API~\cite{express}.
  \item \textbf{Database:} SQLite through Node's built-in \texttt{node:sqlite}
        module~\cite{nodesqlite}: one file, no separate database server.
  \item \textbf{Tools:} Git and GitHub, npm, Vite HTTPS dev server for testing on phones,
        Razorpay test-mode dashboard, oxlint, and the built-in \texttt{node:test} runner.
  \item \textbf{Libraries:} Tailwind CSS~4, Lucide icons and Axios for the UI; ZXing-C++
        compiled to WebAssembly for barcode reading~\cite{zxing,shapedetect}; JsBarcode
        for bill barcodes; the Web Share API, a web app manifest and a service worker for
        sharing and installation~\cite{pwa}; the Razorpay Node SDK, uuid and dotenv on the
        server.
\end{itemize}

\section{Implementation Details}

\subsection{Module 1: Customer App and Barcode Scanning}
After a phone-number sign-in the customer lands on a home screen (bag total, showrooms,
recent receipts). The camera opens only on the \emph{Scan} screen. It uses the rear camera
at 1920$\times$1080 and decodes a wide central band every 90\,ms, plus the full frame every
fourth pass for tags held off-centre. Decoding uses the native \texttt{BarcodeDetector}
where available (Chrome)~\cite{shapedetect}, and otherwise ZXing-C++ compiled to
WebAssembly~\cite{zxing}, which the app serves itself and loads only when the scanner opens.
Only linear formats are enabled (EAN-13/8~\cite{gs1}, UPC, Code 128/39, ITF), so a QR code on
the same tag is never picked up. Repeat reads within 2.2\,s are ignored. A beep, a haptic
tick and an \emph{Undo} action confirm each scan, and manual entry and catalog search
work without a camera.

\subsection{Module 2: GST Pricing and Unified Checkout}
All arithmetic uses integer paise. For GST-exclusive prices, tax is
$\mathrm{round}(\text{subtotal}\times\text{rate})$. Real tags print an MRP that
\emph{includes} tax, so the line total is exactly $\text{MRP}\times\text{qty}$ and the taxable
value is $\text{MRP}\div(1+\text{rate})$. For example, a Rs.~2999 polo bills as Rs.~2541.53
taxable + Rs.~457.47 GST. Tax is summed per slab, split into CGST and SGST, and rounded to
the rupee, and invoice numbers run per financial year (\texttt{FC/2627/000001}). For online
payment the server prices the bag, creates the Razorpay order, and verifies
$\text{HMAC-SHA256}(\textit{order\_id}\,|\,\textit{payment\_id})$ in constant
time~\cite{hmac,razorpay} before saving the invoice and reducing stock in one SQLite
transaction. Replayed callbacks return the same receipt.

\subsection{Module 3: Cash Desk and End-of-Day Drawer}
Choosing cash issues a \emph{pending} invoice with a Code~128 bill barcode. The cashier
(signed in by email and password) records the opening cash, then finds the bill by scanning
it or typing the customer's phone number. Entering the cash received shows the
\emph{change to return} with a note split: Rs.~5000 for a Rs.~3894 bill gives Rs.~1106 as
$2\times500+100+5+1$. Short payments are rejected. The drawer panel shows
\emph{opening cash + cash sales}. At closing, the counted cash is compared with that figure
to report any overage or shortage, and a printable day report is produced. The customer's
receipt turns \emph{Paid} as soon as the cash is recorded. Each cashier has their own drawer: a sidebar lists
every desk opened today with its live cash total, and a cashier starting a shift can take over
the counted float of the colleague who closed last.

\subsection{Module 4: Authentication and Database}
Customers sign in with a 10-digit mobile number; cashiers with an email and a salted scrypt
password hash~\cite{scrypt}. Sessions are HMAC-signed tokens (30 days for customers, 12 hours
for cashiers), role guards keep each role in its own API area, and login endpoints are
rate-limited. Table~\ref{tab:db} lists the schema.

\subsection{Module 5: FlashCart Points}
A customer earns one point for every Rs.~50 actually paid (points come only after a bill is
paid, so a pending cash bill earns nothing until the cashier collects it), and one point is
worth Rs.~1 on a later bill. At checkout a slider lets the customer apply points up to
\emph{half} of the bill; the server re-prices the bag and caps the request against both the
balance and that limit. Points are treated like a voucher rather than a discount: the invoice
total and its CGST/SGST are unchanged, and only the \emph{amount due} that goes to Razorpay or
the cash desk is reduced. Every movement (earn, redeem, credit back, reversal) is written to a
ledger with the running balance, which the \emph{You} tab shows as history.

\subsection{Module 6: Returns, Refunds and Credit Notes}
Scanning a \emph{paid} bill at the cash desk opens a returns panel instead of the cash
collector. The cashier picks items and quantities (food items are excluded, and only the
quantity not yet returned is offered), a reason, and whether to refund in cash or back to the
card/UPI the bill was paid with. The server first \emph{quotes} the credit note, then
\emph{processes} it. Each returned unit gets its exact pro-rata share of the line's taxable
value and GST, so a full return reverses the invoice to the paisa and GST is credited slab by
slab; the credit note is numbered per financial year (\texttt{FC/CN/2627/000001}). Points
redeemed on the returned portion come back as points, points earned on it are reversed, the
items are restocked and the bill is marked partly or fully refunded, all in one SQLite
transaction. Returns are accepted for 30 days, and a cash refund can never exceed the cash in
that drawer, where it is recorded and reduces the end-of-day total.

\subsection{Module 7: Account Insights, Sharing and the Installable App}
The \emph{You} tab calls one summary endpoint that aggregates the customer's bills into
monthly spend for the last six months, the favourite showroom, the most-bought items, recent
refunds, and Food Court items bought before that can be re-ordered with one tap. Every receipt
and credit note carries a share path signed with HMAC-SHA256; the \emph{Share}, \emph{WhatsApp}
and \emph{Copy link} buttons use it, and anyone opening the link sees the bill read-only. The
app ships a web app manifest, icons and a service worker that caches the application shell
(never the API), so it installs from the browser and opens offline; a sun/moon toggle and a
System/Light/Dark setting switch the whole palette, and receipts always print in light.

\section{Software Development Life Cycle}\label{sec:sdlc}
We followed an \textbf{iterative and incremental (agile)} SDLC~\cite{sommerville}
(Figure~\ref{fig:sdlc}). Each iteration went through requirements, design, build, test, demo
and review, and the review feedback set the next scope: iteration~1 (weeks 1--6) built the MVP,
iteration~2 (weeks 7--8) added sign-in, SQLite and the cash desk, iteration~3 (weeks 9--10)
added the barcode engine, real products and MRP pricing, and iteration~4 (weeks 11--12) added
FlashCart points, returns with credit notes, receipt sharing, spend insights, multi-cashier
drawers and the installable app with dark mode.

\subsection{Class Diagram}
Figure~\ref{fig:class} is the domain model. A \emph{Customer} (with a points balance) owns
shopping sessions; a session is composed of \emph{CartLine}s that refer to \emph{Product}s,
carries the points chosen for redemption, and checks out as at most one \emph{Transaction}
(bill). A transaction records the amount due after points, the points earned and its refund
state, is collected within a \emph{CashShift} opened by a \emph{Cashier}, and may be refunded
by several \emph{CreditNote}s, each issued in a shift. Every points movement is a
\emph{LoyaltyLedgerEntry} of the customer. Transactions and credit notes use the stateless
\emph{PricingEngine}, \emph{PaymentGateway} and \emph{LoyaltyService} services, and the
\emph{InsightsService} reads them to build the spend summary.

\section{Testing Methodology}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
  \item \textbf{Unit Testing:} the GST engine: slab totals, CGST/SGST split, round-off,
        MRP back-calculation, points treated as a voucher (GST unchanged, amount due
        reduced) and pro-rata credit notes (a full return reverses the invoice exactly).
  \item \textbf{Integration Testing:} 25 automated API tests (\texttt{node:test}) on the real
        Express app with an in-memory SQLite database: roles, cart, stock limits, Razorpay
        signatures (forged rejected, replays idempotent), a full cash-desk day, points earned
        only when paid and capped at half the bill, partial and full returns (credit note
        numbering, restock, points credited back and reversed, drawer reduced), the rules that
        refuse food items, over-quantity, unpaid bills and refunds larger than the drawer,
        share links (valid token opens, forged token refused) and multi-desk drawers with
        handover.
  \item \textbf{Performance Testing:} scripted load (90 customers $\times$ 3 scans, 90 cash
        bills, 50 collections, 29 return quotes and refunds, 29 points redemptions and
        summaries) and barcode decode timing on photos of real tags.
  \item \textbf{User Testing:} browser end-to-end runs, a Razorpay test checkout, and real
        tags (Puma polo, Mufti shirt, JPG parfum); the points, return, share-link, dark-mode
        and install flows were walked through in the browser on the real database.
\end{itemize}

\section{Analysis}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
  \item \textbf{Objectives:} all seven met. Scanning, which \emph{failed} in the mid-term MVP,
        now reads real retail tags, real-tag bills equal the printed MRP, and the final
        increment added points, returns and the experience features without changing any
        existing total.
  \item \textbf{Challenges and fixes:} the MVP's QR-oriented scanner missed 1D barcodes, so we
        moved to native / ZXing-C++ decoding. MRP tags include GST, so we added tax-inclusive
        pricing. Data was lost on restart, so we added SQLite. Treating points as a discount
        would have changed the taxable value, so they are modelled as a voucher that reduces
        only the amount due. A naive refund of the line total broke on partial returns, so each
        returned unit takes its pro-rata share of taxable value and tax in paise. A cash refund
        once took a small drawer negative, so refunds are now limited to the cash in the drawer.
\end{itemize}

\chapter{Conclusion}
% =====================================================================

\section{Summary of Work}
FlashCart delivers a working unified checkout for multi-showroom retail. Customers sign in
with a phone number, scan real barcodes into one bag, pay once by UPI, card or cash, earn
FlashCart points and spend them on the next bill. Cashiers get a focused cash desk with change
calculation, returns with GST credit notes, a view of every drawer on the floor and a
reconciled end-of-day total. Receipts are shareable, the app installs on the phone with a dark
mode, data is persisted in SQLite, and the rules are covered by 25 automated tests.

\section{Key Findings}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
  \item \textbf{Finding 1:} the EAN-13 codes already on tags are enough to run checkout.
  \item \textbf{Finding 2:} pricing must accept MRP-inclusive tags.
  \item \textbf{Finding 3:} server-side pricing and signature checks keep the client untrusted.
  \item \textbf{Finding 4:} loyalty points fit cleanly into a GST invoice only when they are
        modelled as a payment instrument (voucher), not as a discount on the taxable value.
  \item \textbf{Finding 5:} a return is a first-class document: a pro-rata credit note per
        slab, restocking and the points ledger must move together, or the drawer, stock and
        balances drift apart.
\end{itemize}

\section{Future Work and Recommendations}
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
  \item \textbf{Inventory management:} a per-showroom inventory module for goods received,
        transfers and adjustments. It would add low-stock alerts and reorder suggestions,
        batch and expiry tracking for food, and an admin panel to add products (barcode, cost,
        MRP, GST, type), plus sales analytics and a daily stock reconciliation.
  \item \textbf{Hardware smart cart:} a physical trolley with a mounted barcode scanner (or
        RFID reader), a Raspberry Pi / ESP32 and a touchscreen, joined to the same FlashCart
        session over Wi-Fi. A load cell (HX711) would check the measured weight against the
        scanned items to catch unscanned goods, and the customer could pay on the cart itself.
  \item \textbf{Security and payments:} SMS OTP for phone sign-in, Razorpay webhooks so an
        order settles if the customer closes the tab, and live gateway refunds (the desk
        currently records the gateway refund reference in test mode).
  \item \textbf{Loyalty:} tiers and expiry for points, and showroom-sponsored offers.
  \item \textbf{Scale:} PostgreSQL, cloud deployment and a pilot in one mall wing.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Final Remarks}
The project covered the full software engineering cycle, from real retail requirements and UML
models to tested increments. Letting demo feedback reshape the design made FlashCart simpler
and more useful, and the final increment showed that the server-owned pricing model could
absorb points, refunds and credit notes without a single change to existing invoices.
